# Session 2026-07-23

Scope: Tune daytime sky-disc colors for a more natural appearance at higher sky opacity.

- Topic: Moderate daytime sky-disc chroma
  - Decision: Move the daytime horizon and haze colors toward pale blue, reduce the zenith and Rayleigh blue targets, and lower the broad daytime blue contribution.
  - Rationale: The previous combination produced an overly saturated blue zenith and a pale white horizon when `--sky-opacity` was increased. Keeping the existing altitude and solar-angle model preserves the useful directional shading while making the high-opacity result closer to a real daytime sky.

- Topic: Soften low-sun tint
  - Decision: Reduce sunset strength and use a less clipped orange-red target while retaining the existing low-altitude and solar-direction gating.
  - Rationale: The sunset effect should remain visible near the horizon without turning the sky disc into a strongly red band.

- Topic: Reduce low-altitude haze brightness
  - Decision: Lower the haze target brightness and reduce its minimum and maximum blend strengths.
  - Rationale: Even after the daytime color rebalance, the low-altitude haze lifted the horizon toward white when the disc opacity was increased. A cooler, darker haze keeps the horizon blue without flattening the altitude gradient.

- Topic: Restore sunrise and sunset strength
  - Decision: Restore the previous sunset target color and blend strength while keeping the reduced low-altitude haze.
  - Rationale: The haze adjustment addressed the daytime horizon whiteness, but reducing the sunset contribution at the same time made sunrise and sunset too weak. These controls affect different visual concerns and should remain independent.

- Topic: Slight green shift from reference image
  - Decision: Raise the daytime green contribution slightly and lower the blue contribution in the horizon, zenith, haze, and Rayleigh target colors.
  - Rationale: `shot1.png` has a subtle green-cyan daytime sky. A small coordinated shift across the daytime terms matches that reference without changing the sunset colors or making the sky visibly green.

- Topic: Narrow sunrise and sunset altitude range
  - Decision: Change the sunset transition from `2-6°` to `0-4°` solar altitude.
  - Rationale: Keep the warm tint negligible above `4°`, increase it smoothly between `0°` and `4°`, and reach the maximum at `0°` as requested.

- Topic: Further green shift
  - Decision: Increase the daytime green component slightly and reduce the daytime blue component slightly across the base, haze, and Rayleigh target colors.
  - Rationale: Apply the requested additional green shift while leaving the sunset model and its altitude range unchanged.

- Topic: Remove solar-direction sunset suppression
  - Decision: Set the solar-direction sunset factor to `1.0`, removing the previous one-third reduction at the exact solar direction.
  - Rationale: Test the requested stronger sunrise and sunset appearance while preserving the existing solar-altitude and low-view-altitude gating.

- Topic: Hide sky disc during fast-mode interaction rendering
  - Decision: Add an explicit compositor flag that suppresses the sky disc and ground reset during viewport interaction fast-mode, while retaining the normal background and fast overlays.
  - Rationale: The fast-mode pipeline disabled clouds but still passed `scene.sky_disc_image` to the compositor. Passing `None` alone would have triggered the compositor's black-disc fallback, so the visibility state must be explicit and part of the composite cache key.

- Topic: Replace haze with low-altitude whitening
  - Decision: Remove the fixed haze-color blend and replace it with a view-altitude-only interpolation toward a pale, near-white sky color.
  - Rationale: Test a simpler model in which the zenith blue naturally fades toward white as the viewing altitude decreases, without a separate haze color or solar-altitude dependency. Sunrise and sunset remain separate corrections.

- Topic: Reduce low-altitude whitening
  - Decision: Move the low-altitude whitening target from near-white toward the daytime zenith color.
  - Rationale: The first direct whitening trial made the horizon too bright. Keeping the altitude gradient but using a cooler, darker target should retain the whiteward transition without overwhelming the sky color.

- Topic: Further reduce low-altitude whitening
  - Decision: Move the whitening target one more step toward the zenith color.
  - Rationale: The previous reduction still left the horizon brighter than desired; a smaller target shift preserves the gradient while reducing the remaining white impression.

- Topic: Move whitening onset toward the horizon
  - Decision: Reduce the maximum whitening strength and apply a squared `low_altitude` falloff.
  - Rationale: The linear falloff affected too broad an altitude range. A squared curve keeps the effect concentrated near the horizon while preserving a small whiteward transition there.

- Topic: Add subtle low-horizon warm haze
  - Decision: Add a weak warm tint limited to the lowest `3°` of viewing altitude and gated by low solar altitude.
  - Rationale: Match the supplied landscape reference's slight reddish haze immediately above the sea without broadening the existing directional sunset effect or tinting the daytime sky.

- Topic: Increase sunset color saturation after green sky shift
  - Decision: Lower `SUNSET_RGB` green and blue components from `(1.00, 0.48, 0.14)` to `(1.00, 0.40, 0.10)`.
  - Rationale: The greener daytime base made the previous sunset target appear too close to white when blended. A slightly redder target restores separation without changing sunset strength or altitude gating.

- Topic: Broaden low-horizon warm haze at low solar altitude
  - Decision: From solar altitude `4°` to `0°`, expand the warm haze's viewing-altitude range from about `3°` to `4°` and scale its maximum strength from `1.0` to `1.2`. Keep the existing twilight fade below `0°`.
  - Rationale: Match the requested broader and slightly stronger near-sea haze at the horizon while keeping the effect limited and making it fade with the same twilight factor as the zenith color below the horizon.

- Topic: Extend low-horizon warm haze range
  - Decision: Set the normal viewing-altitude range to `0-4°` and the low-solar-altitude expansion to a maximum of `0-6°`.
  - Rationale: Broaden the near-horizon effect as requested while retaining the existing 20% maximum strength increase and twilight fade.

- Topic: Increase low-horizon warm haze strength
  - Decision: Increase the maximum solar-altitude strength scale at `0°` from `1.2` to `1.5`.
  - Rationale: Apply the requested 50% increase while preserving the `4-6°` viewing-altitude range and below-horizon twilight fade.
