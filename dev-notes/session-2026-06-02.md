# 2026-06-02 session log

Scope: Discuss water-surface rendering semantics and a possible spec/design shift from dot-based to outline-based appearance.

- Topic: Water surface visualization semantics
  - Decision: For preview, switch the renderer from filled dots to thin flattened outline markers while keeping the underlying sampled point model unchanged.
  - Rationale: This reduces the "bowl" illusion at low viewpoints without changing data acquisition or cache behavior. The change is easy to revert because it is isolated to the terrain renderer.
- Topic: Water surface sample density
  - Decision: Halve the azimuth sampling density by changing the default compact-surface step from 2 degrees to 4 degrees, with larger surfaces moving from 1/0.5 degree to 2/1 degree.
  - Rationale: This reduces the number of water markers while preserving the same sampling model and cache key structure.
- Topic: Water surface alpha
  - Decision: Raise the baseline water-surface opacity to make the layer more prominent, and keep the renderer free of an extra alpha multiplier.
  - Rationale: This keeps the visual change aligned with the user-facing opacity control and avoids a separate rendering-only boost knob.
- Topic: Water surface orientation
  - Decision: Rotate the water markers tangentially again so the outline reads like the projection of a circle on a ground-parallel plane.
  - Rationale: The user wants a presentation that behaves like a horizontal disk projected into the view, which is better approximated by a tangential long axis plus distance-based foreshortening.
- Topic: Water surface screen orientation
  - Decision: Drop per-point rotation and keep the marker horizontal in screen space, letting distance-only foreshortening carry the perspective cue.
  - Rationale: The previous directional rotation made edge markers look upright instead of collapsing into near-horizontal slivers near the horizon.
- Topic: Water surface fast-mode sampling
  - Decision: In viewport interaction fast-mode, ignore render resolution and force the coarsest 4-degree azimuth sampling.
  - Rationale: This keeps fast redraws inexpensive and consistent with other fast-mode simplifications.
- Topic: Water surface global sampling
  - Decision: Use the coarsest 4-degree azimuth sampling for all render sizes, not just fast-mode.
  - Rationale: The user preferred one uniform presentation across resolutions, and the coarse step keeps the water overlay visually consistent and inexpensive.
