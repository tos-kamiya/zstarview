# Development session 2026-07-16

Scope: Reduce duplicate PLATEAU LOD2 RoofSurface outlines in zstarview.

- Topic: Contained same-height RoofSurface filtering
  - Decision: Keep the cache format unchanged and filter at runtime. For each building, compare projected RoofSurface rings; when a smaller ring is fully contained by a larger ring and their mean elevations differ by no more than 2m, omit the smaller ring.
  - Rationale: PLATEAU provides semantically valid individual roof faces, but adjacent or nested faces can produce repeated bright lines when every face boundary is emitted. This heuristic preserves separate roof tiers while removing same-level interior fragments.
  - Validation: The focused urban-outline tests pass 15 tests, including a regression test for contained same-height surfaces.

- Topic: Measure containment filter impact
  - Result: In the inspected 13103 tile, 13,068 RoofSurface rings became 13,018, affecting 43 of 1,143 buildings. The Tokyo Tower itself remained at 79 rings, so the visual effect is expected to be difficult to distinguish there.
  - Finding: The dominant brightness is likely caused by shared or nearly coincident edges between adjacent roof faces rather than fully contained faces. A later step should deduplicate projected edges or merge overlapping outline segments.

- Topic: Estimate same-height RoofSurface reduction upper bound
  - Result: If each building retains only one face per consecutive mean-elevation group, the 13,068 surfaces in the inspected tile would reduce to 2,303 at a 2m tolerance, 1,668 at 5m, 1,367 at 10m, and 1,241 at 20m. The Tokyo Tower would reduce from 79 faces to 7 at 2-10m.
  - Caution: This is an intentionally aggressive upper bound. It removes separate adjacent roof faces that happen to share an elevation, so retaining one arbitrary polygon per height group would damage building width and silhouette. Same-height groups should instead be merged into an outer boundary or deduplicated at the edge level.

- Topic: Merge same-height RoofSurface boundaries
  - Decision: Implement runtime projected-edge merging without changing the cache. Same-height surface groups use quantized shared-edge cancellation, boundary-loop stitching, and inner-loop suppression. This keeps the outer boundary of connected roof groups instead of selecting one arbitrary source polygon.
  - Validation: The Tokyo Tower sample changes from 79 RoofSurface rings to 15 merged boundary rings. Focused urban-outline tests pass 16 tests.

- Topic: Tighten same-height tolerance
  - Decision: Reduce `ROOF_SURFACE_ELEVATION_TOLERANCE_M` from 2m to 0.5m to limit merging to more precisely aligned roof surfaces.

- Topic: Distance-dependent RoofSurface tolerance
  - Decision: Use 0.5m within 1km, linearly interpolate from 0.5m to 2m between 1km and 3km, and cap at 2m beyond 3km. Pass the per-building tolerance into the runtime surface-group merge.
  - Rationale: Preserve nearby detail while reducing distant RoofSurface fragmentation that is not visually distinguishable.

- Topic: Revise distance-dependent tolerance
  - Decision: Remove the 0.5m near-distance floor. Use a tolerance of 0m at the observation point, increase linearly with distance, reach 3m at 3km, and cap at 3m beyond 3km.

- Topic: Shift distance-dependent tolerance start
  - Decision: Start at -1m at the observation point, reach 0m at 1km and 3m at 4km, then cap at 3m. When the computed tolerance is negative, skip RoofSurface merging and draw each surface independently.
