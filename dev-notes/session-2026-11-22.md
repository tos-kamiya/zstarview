- Topic: Replace diagonal cloud cutout lines with alt/az-fixed round holes
  - Decision: Add `--cloud-stripe holes[,RADIUS]` and make `holes,4` the default; keep `cutout[,COUNT]` as an option. Holes sit at the 30 above-horizon vertices of a truncated icosahedron fixed in alt/az, with a pentagon centred on the zenith and one zenith-pentagon vertex facing north. The radius is in pixels of the 513x513 cloud raster (constant on screen across FOV changes) with a 1 px anti-aliased rim.
  - Rationale: The concern is moderately thick cloud that does not read as cloud. A round clear area gives an area-to-area comparison that survives the smooth upscale, while a 1 px screen-fixed line blurs into a low-contrast band. Sky-fixed points move with the sky when panning and stay still over time, so they read as reference points rather than a screen pattern. The zenith-pentagon orientation puts rings at about 69.9/46.6/31.0/9.9 degrees, so no row lies on the horizon.
  - Alternatives Considered: Equatorial anchoring (holes would drift with diurnal motion and be confused with stars); angular hole radius (holes would become huge when zoomed in and vanish when zoomed out); geodesic subdivisions for tunable density (kept as a follow-up if 23-degree spacing proves too sparse).
  - Implementation: `CloudVoxelMarkStyle` replaces `cloud_voxel_cutout_count` through runtime options, both GUI controllers, export-image and replay. `project_altaz_to_cloud_image()` is the forward counterpart of `inverse_project_disc()` and is tested for round-trip agreement. Replay now applies the marks with the viewer's `view_center` and `content_fov_deg`.
  - Compatibility: Saved profiles that store `cutout,16` keep the stripes; users switch via the startup dialog or `--cloud-stripe holes`.
  - Validation: Full pytest (1852 passed) and mypy (no issues). Compared `zstarview-export-image Tokyo --cloud-stripe holes|cutout`; holes are clearly visible over moderately thin cloud and invisible on clear sky.
  - Housekeeping: A repository-wide `ruff check --select I --fix` also reordered pre-existing imports in about 30 unrelated files; those were restored with `git checkout` at the user's request and the hole changes were re-applied by hand so the diff stays limited to this feature. Re-ran full pytest (1852 passed) and mypy (no issues).

- Topic: Denser, smaller cloud holes (sunflower trial)
  - Decision: Replace the truncated-icosahedron hole centres with a sunflower (golden-angle) spiral of `CLOUD_VOXEL_HOLE_COUNT = 126` points over the upper hemisphere, with `sin(alt)` evenly spaced so each point covers equal sky area.
  - Rationale: The user wanted more, smaller holes. A subdivided icosahedron (geodesic frequency 5/7) gave the density, but its rows break and tilt relative to the horizon (screenshot shot4.png), which felt unsettling. The sunflower layout keeps even spacing (about 13 degrees for 126 points) with no lattice seams; it is centred on the zenith, so it reads as organised around the observer.
  - Alternatives Considered: Geodesic sphere vertices, frequency 5 (126 above horizon) or 7 (246); rejected for the jagged rows. Concentric equal-altitude rings remain an option if exact altitude alignment is wanted.

- Topic: Concentric equal-altitude hole rings, radius 2 px
  - Decision: Lay out the 126 holes as a zenith point plus seven equal-altitude rings (78, 66, ..., 6 degrees; 5, 11, 15, 20, 23, 25, 26 points), rotating ring k by k golden angles; reduce the default radius to 2 px (`holes,2`).
  - Rationale: The user wanted holes at the same altitude as seen from the observer, forming concentric circles around the zenith. The sunflower spiral gives every point a different altitude. Ring spacing (12 degrees) and along-ring spacing (13 to 15 degrees) stay close, so density remains even; the lowest ring sits half a step above the horizon. The 2 px radius was readable over moderately thin cloud in the 900 px comparison renders.
  - Alternatives Considered: Sunflower spiral (rejected: no equal-altitude rows); a half-step offset on alternate rings (tried first; rejected after the user asked for a sunflower feel, because aligned spokes read as radial lines); no offset (radial grid).
