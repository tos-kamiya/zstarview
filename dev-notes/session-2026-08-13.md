- Topic: GMN publication-delay display policy
  - Decision: Specify a 24-hour meteor window ending at the latest GMN
    observation available at or before the display time. Show its age as
    `M {count}, {oldest}-{newest}h ago`, using hours only.
  - Rationale: GMN trajectory publication commonly trails observation by one
    or more days, so a window ending at real time is often empty. A relative
    range communicates the actual age compactly without implying live data.

- Topic: Implement delayed GMN observation windows
  - Decision: Search indexed daily files newest-first for up to seven days,
    then load only the additional files needed to cover the 24 hours ending at
    the newest observation at or before the display time. Store both the
    requested display time and selected window in `MeteorWindowResult`.
  - Rationale: Progressive discovery avoids routinely downloading a full week
    of large trajectory summaries, while the bounded lookback prevents an
    unbounded historical scan. Keeping both times in the result makes status
    ages deterministic for past views and lets rendering fade relative to the
    selected data window rather than publication delay.
  - Alternatives Considered: Loading the entire seven-day search range before
    choosing a window was simpler, but would add unnecessary network and parse
    cost on every cold-cache update.

- Topic: Meteor trail fade reference
  - Decision: Fade GMN meteor trails from the display time, not `window_end`.
    Opacity decreases linearly with observation age so that 48 hours is 50%
    and 96 hours is 0.
  - Rationale: Window-end fading hid publication delay. Age-from-display
    fading makes older observation windows visibly thinner.
  - Alternatives Considered: Keeping a plateau until 18 hours was rejected
    because the requested curve is a single linear ramp through 50% at 48h.

- Topic: Meteor trail fade floor
  - Decision: Keep the display-time linear fade through 50% at 48 hours, but
    clamp age opacity at `0.2` instead of fading to zero.
  - Rationale: A delayed 24-hour window such as Budapest `M 100, 97-72h ago`
    was nearly invisible because the oldest trails reached 0% and even the
    newest were only 25% before the layer opacity was applied.

- Topic: Meteor trail fade curve
  - Decision: Fade linearly from 100% at 0 hours to 30% at 72 hours, then
    hold 30% for older trails.
  - Rationale: The previous 50%-at-48h curve with a 20% floor still left a
    delayed Budapest window too faint. Reaching the floor later and keeping
    it higher makes 72-97 hour trails remain readable.

- Topic: Meteor trail full-opacity hold
  - Decision: Hold 100% for the first 24 hours from display time, then fade
    linearly over the next 72 hours to 30% and hold that floor.
  - Rationale: GMN high-level trajectories are typically registered at
    least about a day after the observation, so a live view almost never
    contains 0-hour-old trails. A ramp that started immediately at 0 hours
    would therefore never reach 100%. Holding full opacity through 24 hours
    lets the newest published window render at the intended peak. After
    that, older delayed windows such as Budapest `97-72h ago` still fade
    toward 30% so age remains visible, without disappearing.
