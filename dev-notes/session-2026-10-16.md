- Topic: Gaps caused by historical selection policy
  - Finding: Native cloud candidates start at acquisition time but expire 30 minutes after source observation time. Source publication and acquisition delay therefore consumes most of the nominal validity window. A new result arriving after the previous source expires creates gaps despite uninterrupted GUI operation.
  - Evidence: Local metadata contains a 2026-10-14 14:30Z observation acquired at 14:56:08Z, expiring at 15:00Z; the next 14:50Z observation was acquired at 15:07:40Z. Minute coverage consequently omits 15:01 through 15:07Z. These boundaries match the user's listing.
  - Follow-up: Distinguish retrospective source-time reconstruction from reconstruction of data already acquired at each historical instant. Source-time selection would suit retrospective weather playback; preserving the previous displayed source would suit GUI-like acquisition-time playback. Do not silently join ranges or change this policy without documenting the semantic change.

- Topic: Relax cloud replay coverage to follow acquisitions

  - Rationale: This approximates the GUI's retained display during delayed or failed updates without allowing the final cache revision to claim coverage indefinitely. Acquisition metadata cannot prove continuous GUI operation across long gaps, so document that limitation rather than imply new observations every minute.

  - Finding: The current GUI schedules cloud refresh at successful completion plus CLOUD_UPDATE_INTERVAL (10 minutes). Himawari lookup searches backward in 10-minute observation slots. An observation timestamp predicts the next nominal slot, but does not specify when the provider will finish publishing the required tiles. Timestamp-aware polling could improve scheduling but is outside this replay-only change.
